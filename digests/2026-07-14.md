Three projects cleared today’s hardware-first promotion bar. [Open Pedalboard](https://github.com/pedalboard/pedalboard-hw) is the strongest result: a product-level, multi-repository CM4/CM5 + RP2040 pedal platform with editable KiCad electronics, a dedicated PCM1863/PCM5242 soundcard, a display board, OpenSCAD mechanics, manufacturing automation, and independent real-time control firmware. No ranked project appears in the WebGPT publication tracker or selected-project list. Plinky and Open Pedalboard are absent from the common anti-repeat index. ### 1) [pedalboard/pedalboard-hw ecosystem](https://github.com/pedalboard/pedalboard-hw) — **STRONG_PASS**

**Lane:** Standalone multi-board guitar/audio processor and MIDI control platform

#### Technical summary

Open Pedalboard is a modular floor processor split across five useful repositories:

- [Mainboard](https://github.com/pedalboard/pedalboard-hw): Raspberry Pi CM4/CM5 audio host plus an RP2040 control domain.
- [PCM1863/PCM5242 soundcard](https://github.com/pedalboard/pedalboard-soundcard): two-channel differential ADC/DAC HAT.
- [Display/control board](https://github.com/pedalboard/pedalboard-display): eight 12-pixel RGB rings, two extra LEDs and two OLED ports.
- [Mechanical source](https://github.com/pedalboard/pedalboard-case): OpenSCAD parts, drill jig, panel template, DXF and CNC G-code.
- [RP2040 firmware](https://github.com/pedalboard/pedalboard-midi): Rust/RTIC real-time MIDI/control firmware with persistent configuration and host tests.

The mainboard supports MIDI-only operation without a CM module or combined MIDI/audio operation with CM4/CM5 and the codec HAT. The project claims approximately 2 ms latency with ELK Audio OS; that is a platform claim, not an independently reproduced measurement in this run.

#### Why it matters

This is closer to a reusable product architecture than a one-off effect board. Linux handles high-level audio processing and networking; RP2040 starts immediately and owns footswitches, encoders, expression inputs, displays, LED rings and MIDI. That partition avoids making basic control responsiveness depend on Linux boot or scheduling, and it gives a clean path from a low-cost MIDI controller SKU to a full DSP product.

#### Implementation highlights

- CM4/CM5 audio computer plus independent RP2040 control processor.
- PCM1863 ADC and PCM5242 DAC soundcard with differential stereo I/O.
- Six footswitches, two push encoders, two expression inputs, eight LED-ring ports, two OLED ports and config EEPROM.
- DIN, TRS, USB and internal/header MIDI options.
- 6–28 V DC input, 3 A buck stage and USB-powered MIDI-only option.
- Rust/RTIC firmware with MIDI-CI Property Exchange, 64 kB wear-levelled flash storage, EEPROM runtime state, YAML setlists, host tests and integration tests.
- KiCad CLI/KiBot automation for ERC, DRC, BOM, placement, Gerbers, STEP, PDFs, interactive BOM and manufacturer-specific packages.
- CM5 verification recorded in the 4.0.1-RC changelog; earlier mainboard revisions have manufactured lineage back to 1.0.1.

#### Hardware and electronics notes

**Hardware modifiability**

- Editable KiCad project, hierarchical schematics and mainboard PCB source: **verified**.
- Dedicated soundcard schematic/PCB project and automated checks: **verified at repository level**.
- Display PCB source: **verified at repository level**.
- BOM and PnP/manufacturing generation: **verified**.
- Enclosure/panel source: **OpenSCAD, STL, DXF, PDF template and CNC G-code verified**.
- Calibration/test notes: firmware tests and hardware ERC/DRC exist; a complete analog production-test specification was not found.
- Hardware licence: **CERN-OHL-P-2.0**; OSHWA mainboard certification **CH000023**.

The architecture is credible, but the mainboard’s two-layer USB 2.0/CM connector implementation deserves a real impedance/return-path audit before a new spin. The project itself still lists input protection as TODO. The wide 6–28 V input and 3 A converter also make conducted noise, switch-node placement, thermal rise and pedalboard ground-loop behaviour first-order risks. For the soundcard, differential I/O does not remove the need to verify input overload, guitar-source impedance, anti-alias behaviour, headphone/line drive, codec clocking and CM4/CM5 ground-current coupling.

#### Platform relevance

- **Very high:** programmable multi-effect pedalboard.
- **Very high:** Linux + MCU hybrid instrument/control architecture.
- **High:** reusable PCM1863/PCM5242 analog front end.
- **High:** commercial derivative potential because the hardware licence is permissive.
- **Medium:** deterministic hard-real-time DSP; the audio layer depends on the Linux/ELK integration rather than an MCU-only path.

#### Adaptation ideas

1. Replace the generic Linux audio layer with a curated pedal runtime: LV2/CLAP host, fixed graph, watchdog, A/B rootfs and deterministic preset migration.
2. Add a protected high-impedance guitar input, relay true bypass, balanced line mode and measured gain/noise/headroom profiles.
3. Reuse the RP2040 board/firmware as a standalone programmable MIDI controller SKU.
4. Convert the display board and mechanical generator into a family of 4/6/8-switch products.
5. Add factory test firmware for expression ADC linearity, switch/encoder coverage, LED current, MIDI loopback, codec THD+N and full audio-channel gain/phase.

#### Quick engineering assessment

**Promote.** This is the best productisation base in the run. Before fabrication, audit the current mainboard/soundcard revision pair, close the input-protection TODO, verify the exact ELK/kernel/audio configuration and run SI/PI plus analog noise measurements. The source and licensing are good enough that these are engineering tasks, not archaeological reconstruction.

#### Caveats / verification gaps

- The approximately 2 ms latency is project-reported and depends on ELK Audio OS configuration.
- Mainboard 4.0.1 is marked release-candidate, not a final production release.
- Current soundcard fabrication/measurement evidence was not independently confirmed.
- USB 2.0 on two layers can work, but stackup, reference continuity and connector geometry must be checked rather than assumed.
- Input protection remains in the project TODO list.

### 2) [plinkysynth/plinky_public](https://github.com/plinkysynth/plinky_public) — **STRONG_PASS**

**Lane:** Standalone/Eurorack eight-voice touch synthesizer

#### Technical summary

Plinky is an eight-voice pressure/touch instrument based on an STM32L476VGT6. The repository contains current community-developed V3.1 mainboard source, front/back-panel projects, a manufacturing test jig, an earlier production-proven V2 package, firmware, bootloader, desktop emulator and detailed firmware revision history.

Its main value is the integrated control surface: a dense touch grid, LEDs, OLED, encoder, analog/CV connections, MIDI and audio are treated as one instrument rather than as a generic dev board with peripherals attached.

#### Why it matters

The current open hardware is the result of several real production and field-feedback cycles. V3 added onboard MIDI, V3.1 addressed input noise, and the repository retains the test jig used to flash, calibrate CVs and exercise production units. That is much more reusable than a schematic plus hero photograph.

#### Implementation highlights

- Eight-voice synth firmware on STM32L476VGT6.
- Current V3.1 open mainboard plus front/back-panel projects.
- Onboard MIDI in/out in the current hardware line.
- Dedicated bootloader and UF2/STM32 flashing paths.
- STM32CubeIDE project plus a separate no-Cube make path and desktop/WebAssembly emulator material.
- Current B2 firmware includes Plinky+ detection and substantial MIDI, voice-allocation, sequencer, latch, CV-gate and timing fixes.
- Test-jig hardware for flashing, calibration and manufacturing checks.
- Commercial kit/prebuilt lineage provides strong evidence that the design is not fabrication-only.

#### Hardware and electronics notes

**Hardware modifiability**

- Editable KiCad mainboard source: **verified by current V3.1 source directory and project documentation**.
- Editable front/back panel source: **verified**.
- Schematic and PCB source: **present**.
- BOM: **present**, though the root BOM is oriented toward kit completion and should be reconciled with CAD-generated per-revision component data.
- Manufacturing files: production-tested V2 JLC BOM/POS files are archived; current V3.1 source is present.
- Test jig and calibration path: **present**.
- Firmware/bootloader/emulator: **present**.
- Hardware licence: **CERN-OHL-P-2.0**; software **MIT**; graphics **CC BY-SA 4.0**.

The design’s risk concentration is the mixed touch/CV/audio/control surface. Electrode parasitics, LED scan/current noise, display traffic, codec/SAI clocks and Eurorack ground currents can all couple into sensitive input paths. The community’s V3.1 input-noise correction is encouraging but also evidence that layout and grounding are not trivial. A derivative should preserve a known-good electrode stackup and test finger-pressure linearity, false touches, temperature/humidity drift and LED-state-correlated noise.

#### Platform relevance

- **Very high:** standalone expressive synth/control surface.
- **High:** Eurorack/desktop dual-use mechanical and I/O packaging.
- **High:** touch-keyboard and LED-feedback architecture.
- **High:** commercial derivative potential under CERN-OHL-P, with branding separation.
- **Medium:** clean greenfield firmware base; the repository still carries substantial generated STM32 and third-party code.

#### Adaptation ideas

1. Reuse the touch/LED interaction model for a compact wavetable, granular or sampler instrument.
2. Extract the touch scanner, calibration and visual-feedback subsystem as a reusable controller board.
3. Add a modern USB-C power/data implementation and explicit ESD/EMC design review.
4. Replace ad-hoc BOM documentation with revision-locked KiBot outputs and automated assembly packages.
5. Use the existing emulator to develop DSP/UI features before committing new hardware.

#### Quick engineering assessment

**Promote.** Plinky is a rare combination of expressive industrial design, editable source hardware, production tooling and active community firmware. For a derivative, start from current V3.1, not the easier-to-find archived V2 files, and perform a clean local KiCad library/ERC/DRC and BOM audit.

#### Caveats / verification gaps

- The repository is candidly disorganised; revision selection must be explicit.
- The current V3.1 manufacturing package was not independently rebuilt in this run.
- Root BOM links include marketplace sources and are not a production-grade AVL.
- The precise analog/audio performance and EMC behaviour were not measured here.
- Branding and front-panel derivative guidance must be respected even though hardware reuse is permissive.

### 3) [T-DSP hardware ecosystem](https://www.t-dsp.com/) — **REF_PASS**

**Lane:** Modular Teensy/ESP32 audio backplane and TAC5212 codec system

#### Technical summary

T-DSP separates digital processing, codec conversion and connector-specific analog I/O into reusable boards. The [T-DSP Core](https://github.com/t-dsp/t-dsp_core) is a four-layer Teensy 4.1 + ESP32 backplane with USB Audio, MIDI, Ethernet, S/PDIF, UI headers and TDM expansion. The [TAC5212 Pro Audio Module](https://github.com/t-dsp/t-dsp_tac5212_pro_audio_module) is a solderable 50 × 34.5 mm codec module with differential stereo I/O, I²S/TDM and I²C. Additional repositories cover balanced 2×2 I/O, a desktop backplane, microphone arrays and shield adapters.

#### Why it matters

The valuable idea is the physical/electrical codec-module ABI. A codec board can be socketed or soldered into a pedal, mixer, instrument or audio interface while the product board owns jacks, controls and mechanics. This reduces repeated high-risk codec layout work and makes multi-codec TDM systems practical.

#### Implementation highlights

- Teensy 4.1 real-time DSP plus ESP32 wireless/UI partition.
- Standard I²S/TDM, I²C and 2.54 mm module interfaces.
- TAC5212 module with two differential ADC paths and two differential DAC paths.
- Project-stated 118 dB ADC / 119 dB DAC dynamic-range class for TAC5212.
- On-module analog LDO, ferrite input filtering, output TVS protection and buffered digital lines.
- Daisy-chain concept for up to four modules on a tapped ribbon/backplane bus.
- Full KiCad projects, schematic PDFs, STEP models, BOM scripts and KiBot-generated Gerbers/CPL/iBOM.
- Core backplane adds headphone/RCA, S/PDIF, Ethernet, USB host/device, MIDI, SD, OLED/TFT and LED headers.

#### Hardware and electronics notes

**Hardware modifiability**

- Editable KiCad schematic, PCB and project files: **verified** for Core and TAC5212 module.
- BOM, placement and Gerber generation: **verified**.
- Module footprint and 3D/mechanical assets: **verified**.
- Firmware/source: separate T-DSP software repository exists; software was not the focus of this entry.
- Enclosure/panel source: project-specific panels/3D assets exist, but a complete product enclosure was not verified.
- Hardware licence: **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**.
- Build status: **current TAC5212 revision explicitly not yet built**; an earlier board worked but had routing errors and required output-stage redesign.

The module interface needs formal SI/PI constraints before it becomes a dependable ecosystem: allowable BCLK/MCLK rates, trace and ribbon length, module count, source termination, ground-pin allocation, clock-master ownership, address conflicts and analog return-current rules. “Buffered digital outputs” does not automatically make a multi-drop TDM cable robust. The 52-pin module also creates many possible ground loops if analog and digital ground partitioning is copied without a system-level return-current analysis.

#### Platform relevance

- **High:** codec daughterboard/backplane architecture.
- **High:** Teensy multichannel mixer/effects/synth testbed.
- **High:** hardware reuse for prototypes.
- **Low-to-medium:** direct commercial product base due to NC licence.
- **Medium:** fabrication confidence until the current codec revision is built and measured.

#### Adaptation ideas

1. Define and publish an electrical compliance specification for the module/backplane bus.
2. Build a two-module TDM validation fixture measuring jitter, channel crosstalk, THD+N and cable-length margin.
3. Port the codec driver to STM32H7/Daisy or RP2350 while retaining the board ABI.
4. Create guitar/instrument and balanced-line front-end modules with known overload/noise targets.
5. Redraw the architecture under a commercially permissive licence only with explicit permission or a clean independent implementation.

#### Quick engineering assessment

**Reference, prototype and measure.** The source depth is excellent, but the present hardware maturity and NC licence prevent a higher lane. The ecosystem becomes a strong `PASS` candidate once the current TAC5212 board has a verified build, audio measurements and a published bus constraint table.

#### Caveats / verification gaps

- Current TAC5212 revision is unbuilt.
- Earlier functional hardware had documented routing/output-stage problems.
- Hardware licence is non-commercial.
- No independent audio measurements or multi-module bus-margin data were found.
- T-DSP software was featured by the weekly stream on 2026-06-09; this hardware-specific entry is outside the 30-day window but should not be repeated again before 2026-08-13.
