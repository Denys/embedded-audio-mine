# Embedded Audio Mine — 2026-07-17

Three projects cleared the hardware-first promotion bar. [TŒRN](https://github.com/Soundpauli/toern) is today’s best product-shaped discovery: a current, physically shipped Teensy groovebox with editable revision-G KiCad rather than a firmware-only shell. [Tiliqua](https://github.com/apfaudio/tiliqua) is the deeper electronics find: an unusually complete three-PCBA FPGA audio ecosystem. [Acid Drip](https://github.com/lonesoulsurfer/Acid_Drip_Bassline_and_Drum_Synth) is a fresh, highly editable standalone groovebox, but its missing licence and minimal PWM output stages limit it to reference promotion.

All three were absent from the repository’s daily publication tracker, selected-project list, common anti-repeat index, and recent committed digests. The July 14–16 winners were used only as calibration anchors.

## Ranked entries

### 1) [Soundpauli/TŒRN](https://github.com/Soundpauli/toern) — **STRONG_PASS**

**Lane:** Standalone sampler, sequencer, looper and synth

#### Technical summary

TŒRN is a battery-ready Teensy 4.1 instrument built around a custom carrier with SGTL5000 audio, BQ24075 charging, PAM8403 amplification, microSD, TRS MIDI, USB-C, line/mic/headphone connections, a 16×16 WS2812 matrix, four RGB push encoders and capacitive controls. The firmware provides eight sample voices, a three-voice poly synth, two mono synths, live recording, pattern/song storage, filters, bit reduction and reverb.

The repository was still moving on 2026-07-16: the latest commits added USB-serial SD transfer and described stability/audio-quality fixes. The official project page documents a real small-batch product, not just renderings.

#### Why it matters

This is the closest match in today’s pool to the preferred Ksoloti/Polykit archetype: electronics, controls, mechanics, firmware, data-transfer tools and physical-product evidence in one adaptation surface. Its grid-plus-encoder interface is especially reusable for a sampler, live looper, multi-delay controller or compact performance sequencer.

#### Implementation highlights

- Teensy 4.1 with 16 MB PSRAM and SGTL5000 audio path.
- Eight 44.1 kHz/16-bit mono sample voices, each supporting up to roughly 12 seconds, plus three synth channels.
- Live sampling from internal microphone, external microphone or line input.
- Up to 999 patterns and samples, pattern chaining, conditional/probability triggers, MIDI clock and TRS MIDI I/O.
- USB-serial browser/CLI file transfer avoids relying on USB mass-storage mode.
- Firmware explicitly wraps WS2812 updates and increases MIDI serial buffers to reduce display/audio and clock-traffic conflicts.

#### Hardware and electronics notes

**Hardware modifiability:** editable KiCad 9 revision-G schematic and PCB source are present, along with Gerbers and a downloadable revision archive. The repository licence explicitly covers schematics, PCB layouts, Gerbers and BOMs. The official page says laser-cut acrylic enclosure files are included, but the exact revision-G enclosure/BOM/CPL set was not independently reconciled in this run.

The main mixed-signal risks are familiar but material: WS2812 update timing can interrupt or delay audio work; I²C encoder traffic and LED current steps can couple into the SGTL5000 path; the LiPo charge/power path needs thermal and load-step tests; and microphone, line and headphone gains should be measured rather than inferred from the codec. The project’s wrappers show the author is aware of timing interference, but no formal latency, noise, THD+N, battery-runtime or EMC report was found.

Licensing is split cleanly but restrictively: software is MIT; hardware is CC BY-NC 4.0, so commercial derivatives require permission.

#### Platform relevance

- **Very high:** standalone sampler/groovebox/control-surface architecture.
- **Very high:** product-level Teensy 4.1 audio hardware with real controls and power management.
- **High:** reusable live-performance UI and removable-storage workflow.
- **Medium:** commercial adaptation because the hardware licence is non-commercial.

#### Adaptation ideas

1. Replace the single 16×16 matrix with a lower-current scanned matrix or segmented UI while preserving the live workflow.
2. Use its power, MIDI and control architecture as a front end for a multi-delay pedal with per-tap sequencing.
3. Add fixture-driven tests for every encoder, LED, touch input, MIDI port, audio route and charger state.
4. Measure WS2812 refresh against audio callback jitter and move display transport to DMA or a companion MCU if needed.
5. Publish a revision-locked BOM/CPL/enclosure bundle and approved substitutions for the codec, charger and encoders.

#### Quick engineering assessment

**Promote for adaptation, with a manufacturing audit first.** It is unusually complete and current. Before ordering a derivative, freeze revision G, reconcile the CAD/BOM/mechanics, and measure the audio and power paths under worst-case LED activity.

#### Caveats / verification gaps

- No tagged firmware release exists; current users track a fast-moving main branch.
- Hardware is non-commercial despite repeated “open source” wording.
- The site and repository contain a few specification inconsistencies, including mono engine claims versus stereo preview/routing language.
- Formal audio, battery, ESD and EMC validation was not found.

### 2) [apfaudio/Tiliqua ecosystem](https://github.com/apfaudio/tiliqua) — **STRONG_PASS**

**Lane:** Open FPGA audio/CV multitool and reusable multi-board ecosystem

#### Technical summary

Tiliqua combines three separable PCBAs: an R5.1 motherboard/front panel, an R3.0 SoldierCrab ECP5 FPGA SoM, and an R3.5 Eurorack PMOD audio board. The system provides four DC-coupled inputs and four outputs at up to 192 kHz, jack insertion and capacitive sensing, per-jack indicators, 32 MB high-speed PSRAM, USB 2.0 high speed, RP2040-assisted flashing/debug, optoisolated TRS MIDI input, video output and two expansion ports.

The Amaranth gateware/software tree includes oscillators, delays, reverb, pitch shifting, polysynth and soft-CPU/menu examples. Hardware and gateware are principally CERN-OHL-S-2.0, and the project is sold and documented as functioning hardware.

#### Why it matters

Although Eurorack normally ranks below standalone devices, Tiliqua earns an exception for its electronics value. It is a genuine Polykit-like ecosystem: removable compute, reusable multichannel audio/CV front end, motherboard, expansion route, calibration storage and an open toolchain. The SoldierCrab SoM and PMOD audio board are useful independently of the rack product.

#### Implementation highlights

- Lattice ECP5-25 FPGA with open-source synthesis/place-and-route support.
- 32 MB oSPI/HyperRAM tested around 400 MB/s for long audio buffers or video frames.
- Four inputs and four outputs, DC-coupled, ±9 V input and ±8 V output ranges, insertion sensing, touch sensing and calibration EEPROM.
- USB 2.0 high-speed device/host path plus RP2040 JTAG bridge supported by `openFPGALoader`.
- Soft mute across bitstream changes, SI5351A clocking, TRS MIDI and PMOD expansion.
- Active maintenance: Amaranth 0.5.8 dependency update landed on 2026-07-04.

#### Hardware and electronics notes

**Hardware modifiability:** editable KiCad source is present for the R5.1 motherboard/front panel, R3.0 SoldierCrab SoM and R3.5 audio board. PDF schematics, panel data, changelogs and repository-specific sources are linked from the primary electrical-design documentation. Licensing is explicit CERN-OHL-S-2.0 across the main design family.

This is a high-complexity build: fine-pitch FPGA/USB/RAM assembly, 192 kHz mixed-signal layout, multiple rails, USB-C role control, calibration and Eurorack protection are not hobby-board trivialities. A documented early-R5.1 USB-C edge case could weakly back-power VBUS; gateware changed on 2026-06-07 to disable DFP terminations when host mode is off. That mitigation should not replace checking the exact board revision and hardware issue before manufacturing.

#### Platform relevance

- **Exceptional:** reusable FPGA SoM and multichannel audio/CV front end.
- **High:** open USB-audio, MIDI, video and hardware-DSP experimentation.
- **High:** multi-board architecture and calibration/test concepts.
- **Medium:** standalone-instrument fit without a custom carrier/control surface.

#### Adaptation ideas

1. Build a desktop carrier around SoldierCrab plus the audio PMOD, with a Ksoloti-style control surface.
2. Use the four-in/four-out DC-coupled path for matrix delay, spectral routing or audio/CV hybrid processing.
3. Port the soft-mute and calibration architecture to a Daisy/STM32 multicodec board.
4. Add automated production calibration for every channel and store coefficients in the onboard EEPROM.
5. Treat the PMOD interface as a reusable codec daughterboard boundary in future multi-board instruments.

#### Quick engineering assessment

**Promote as a top-tier electronics ecosystem.** It is more reusable than most single-board modules, but a derivative needs FPGA, high-speed USB and mixed-signal manufacturing competence.

#### Caveats / verification gaps

- The OSHWA certificate is for version 1.0 and should not be assumed to certify every R5.1 detail.
- Exact Crowd Supply manufacturing outputs were not compared byte-for-byte with current repository CAD.
- The early-R5.1 USB-C erratum must be checked against any board purchase or derivative.
- Complexity and FPGA toolchain learning cost are substantially higher than Teensy/Daisy projects.

### 3) [lonesoulsurfer/Acid Drip](https://github.com/lonesoulsurfer/Acid_Drip_Bassline_and_Drum_Synth) — **REF_PASS**

**Lane:** Standalone RP2040 acid synth and drum groovebox

#### Technical summary

Acid Drip runs a Mozzi acid voice and sample-based drum engine concurrently on an RP2040. It uses a 320×240 ILI9341 display, sixteen Cherry MX step pads, performance pots, separate synth/drum connections, pattern memory and chaining, accents, glide, scale-aware sequencing, probability-like performance operations and preset drum patterns.

The repository is fresh: its current hardware/firmware upload landed on 2026-07-13. It includes revision-2 editable electronics and front-panel data while the photographs show a built revision-1 unit.

#### Why it matters

The hardware is deliberately simple and therefore easy to read, modify and cost-reduce. It is a good reference for dividing an RP2040 across two musical engines and fitting a dense sequencer UI around inexpensive through-hole controls. Unlike many polished pocket synths, the actual board and panel are editable.

#### Implementation highlights

- Raspberry Pi Pico/RP2040 carrier with Mozzi audio.
- Sixteen direct-connected Cherry MX keys and 2.8-inch TFT.
- Acid sequencer with accent, glide, scale/riff generation, pattern lengths and four-slot chaining.
- Separate drum engine using embedded 8-bit samples and preset patterns.
- Editable main PCB, editable PCB front panel, firmware and CSV BOM.

#### Hardware and electronics notes

**Hardware modifiability:** KiCad 9 main-board schematic, PCB and project files are verified. A CSV BOM is present. The front panel also has editable KiCad schematic/PCB source plus Gerbers. No full enclosure source was verified. Most importantly, no explicit licence file or licence text was found, so the files are source-available but should not be described as legally open hardware.

The output stages are extremely minimal: the acid output is documented as GP15 through 100 Ω and 10 nF, while the drum output uses GP2 through 470 Ω. GP2 is also switched to sync input. A product derivative needs proper reconstruction filtering, buffering, output protection, muting and level/headroom definition. The 100 Ω/10 nF pole is far above the audio band, so it should not be assumed to suppress PWM carrier energy adequately. Switching one pin between output and external sync also deserves contention and ESD analysis.

#### Platform relevance

- **High:** simple standalone groovebox/control layout.
- **High:** editable RP2040 hardware and panel reference.
- **Medium:** reusable firmware/UI ideas.
- **Low-medium:** production audio quality without a redesigned output path.

#### Adaptation ideas

1. Replace both PWM outputs with an I²S DAC/codec and proper line drivers.
2. Add TRS MIDI input/output and isolate clock/sync from the drum-audio pin.
3. Reuse the direct 16-pad layout for a compact delay-tap sequencer or performance looper.
4. Add a parametric enclosure and revision-locked manufacturing pack.
5. Measure PWM carrier, THD+N, output impedance, crosstalk and control-to-audio feedthrough.

#### Quick engineering assessment

**Promote as a fresh hardware reference, not as production-ready open hardware.** The editable CAD is valuable, but the audio front end and licensing must be fixed before serious reuse.

#### Caveats / verification gaps

- No explicit software or hardware licence was found.
- Physical photos are identified as revision 1; the repository says the provided revision 2 looks different.
- No assembled-revision-2 verification, release tag, enclosure CAD or audio measurements were found.
- The firmware depends on Arduino/Mozzi timing behavior and needs worst-case TFT/update testing.
