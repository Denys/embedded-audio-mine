### 1) [Mini Synth](https://ocw.cs.pub.ro/courses/pm/prj2026/bianca.popa1106/mihai.brisculescu) + [MidsoftEntertainment/MiniSynth-public](https://github.com/MidsoftEntertainment/MiniSynth-public) — **PASS**

**Lane:** Portable standalone synth, looper and embedded control surface

#### Technical summary

Mini Synth is a current university hardware project built around a Teensy 4.0. A single custom PCB carries a 25-key switch keyboard, four SN74HC165 input shift registers, two octave buttons, joystick, two pots, OLED, SD module, battery/power circuitry and three parallel I²S sinks: one UDA1334A stereo DAC and two MAX98357A Class-D amplifiers.

The public firmware implements an eight-voice Teensy Audio graph, local UI, scales, persistence, tempo and an in-memory looper. The loop buffer uses roughly 440 KB of DMAMEM; SD is used for user-triggered WAV export rather than in-loop streaming. The main loop is cooperative and non-blocking while audio runs under DMA.

#### Why it matters

This is not polished repository theatre. The board is assembled and working, and the project page records the specific first-revision failure in jack detection: AC-coupled DAC outputs left the tip nodes floating, fixed by adding 10 kΩ pull-downs on the audio side of the coupling capacitors. That kind of correction is more valuable than another immaculate render with no oscilloscope ever harmed.

It also fits the updated hardware rule: a module-based single PCB is legitimate hardware when the integration, controls, power, audio routing and bring-up are documented.

#### Implementation highlights

- Teensy 4.0 at 600 MHz with Teensy Audio Library and DMA-driven I²S.
- Eight-voice configurable polyphony and two synthesis paths.
- 25 local keys, joystick, two pots, octave controls and OLED menu/HUD.
- UDA1334A line/headphone path plus dual MAX98357A speaker amplifiers on a shared I²S bus.
- In-memory mono looper with explicit non-blocking control architecture.
- Diagnostic firmware that keeps amplifiers muted until a deliberate safe test-tone sequence.
- Li-ion charging, boost conversion, battery monitoring and SD WAV export.

#### Hardware and electronics notes

**Evidence split:**

- **Physical build:** verified by the current project page and bring-up log.
- **Schematic:** published as part of the project documentation.
- **Editable EDA:** the PCB is stated to be designed in KiCad and manufactured by Aisler, but the editable KiCad project was not found in the public firmware repository.
- **Fabrication package:** not verified.
- **Firmware:** verified in the public repository; modular subsystem files and the DMA/non-blocking main sketch were inspected.
- **Licence:** no project-level licence was found; bundled third-party library licences do not solve that.

The main engineering concern is unnecessary breakout-module layering. A derivative should consolidate the codec/DAC, speaker amplifiers, SD and power path, define analogue and power grounds deliberately, and replace improvised jack detection with a revision-tested circuit. Triple loads on the same I²S bus also deserve edge-rate and clock-integrity review.

#### Platform relevance

- **Very high:** standalone Teensy instrument and dense local UI.
- **High:** practical looper and safe bring-up architecture.
- **High:** custom PCB integrating commodity modules without a cable nest.
- **Medium:** direct derivative use until editable CAD and licensing appear.

#### Adaptation ideas

1. Replace the three I²S modules with one codec plus explicit line/headphone and Class-D output stages.
2. Keep the 25-key control topology but move shift-register scanning to hardware SPI or DMA-assisted GPIO.
3. Reuse the DMAMEM looper architecture in a compact sampler, delay workstation or pedal prototype.
4. Add fixture-test pads for every key scan chain, audio route, jack detect, battery rail and SD interface.
5. Publish a corrected revision with editable KiCad, BOM/CPL, enclosure and clear hardware/software licences.

#### Quick engineering assessment

**Promote as a current hardware-development reference, not yet as a clean derivative base.** Its strongest value is the combined PCB, firmware and bring-up evidence. The missing source CAD and licence cap the lane at `PASS`.

#### Caveats / verification gaps

- Editable PCB source and fabrication outputs were not located.
- Project-level licence is absent.
- No measured noise, THD+N, speaker power, thermal or battery-runtime report was found.
- Module-level power and grounding need a proper mixed-signal audit.

#### Sources

- [Current university project page](https://ocw.cs.pub.ro/courses/pm/prj2026/bianca.popa1106/mihai.brisculescu)
- [Public firmware repository](https://github.com/MidsoftEntertainment/MiniSynth-public)

### 2) [Brume](https://brume.aftertone.co/) — **PASS**

**Lane:** Software-defined standalone multitimbral instrument and DAW appliance

#### Technical summary

Brume turns a Raspberry Pi Compute Module 5, carrier board and touchscreen into a four-part, 24-voice synthesizer. It provides FM, harmonic/additive, folded-timbral and granular engines feeding a common per-voice filter/modulation/envelope tail. A Rust real-time runtime, touchscreen UI, Lua extension layer and USB gadget path form the instrument.

The developer guide documents a Rust workspace, zero-allocation audio callback contract, preallocated buffers, lock avoidance, workspace tests, cross-deployment and SCHED_FIFO capability setup. End-user deployment starts from Raspberry Pi OS Lite and uses `brumectl` over SSH.

#### Why it matters

Brume is unusual in a useful way: it treats the hardware as a reproducible reference appliance rather than pretending that every musical instrument needs a bespoke PCB. The product boundary is the tested runtime, audio/MIDI gadget behaviour, touchscreen workflow, install tooling and controller-mapping layer.

That is directly relevant to fast product validation. A CM5 reference instrument can validate sound engines, DAW integration and UI before committing to a custom carrier, codec and control surface.

#### Implementation highlights

- Four multitimbral parts, six voices each.
- Four distinct engines: six-operator FM, harmonic scanning, folded timbral and granular.
- State-variable filter, envelopes, per-part modulation and shared effects.
- Rust DSP core with explicit no-allocation/no-blocking audio-path rules.
- Lua sequencing, controller behaviour, drawing and custom FX surface.
- CM5 deployment and update path using cross-build plus atomic service replacement.
- Class-compliant USB audio/MIDI/clock concept through the CM5 IO-board peripheral path.

#### Hardware and electronics notes

**Evidence split:**

- **Physical/reference hardware:** CM5, CM5 IO/carrier board and HDMI touchscreen are explicitly specified; the guide says the CM5 reference rig is regression-tested.
- **Editable EDA:** intentionally absent because Brume uses off-the-shelf hardware.
- **Fabrication package:** not applicable to the reference build.
- **Software source/build:** architecture, source-tree pointers, build, tests and deployment are extensively documented.
- **Licence:** the in-tree DSP core is documented as GPL-3.0-only; exact repository-wide licensing and the GitHub source location should be checked before reuse.

The official pages are inconsistent about whether per-part USB stems are already shipping or planned. Treat stereo USB audio as the conservative baseline until the current release is exercised on hardware.

#### Platform relevance

- **Exceptional:** software-defined instrument and rapid product-validation architecture.
- **High:** Linux real-time audio, CM5 USB gadget and controller abstraction.
- **High:** unusual concept value under the recalibrated mine.
- **Low:** board-level electronics reuse before a custom carrier exists.

#### Adaptation ideas

1. Use Brume as the software/UI prototype for a later custom CM5 carrier with codec, MIDI, balanced I/O and power management.
2. Port one engine and the controller profile layer to an STM32H7/Daisy reference target to define the boundary between Linux and MCU products.
3. Add HARP-style content-addressed total recall and explicit engine/hardware revision identity.
4. Benchmark worst-case xrun rate, scheduling latency, cold boot, USB round-trip latency and thermal throttling.
5. Replace the 10.1-inch screen with a smaller touch display plus four performance encoders for a product-shaped desktop instrument.

#### Quick engineering assessment

**Promote as an unusual buildable architecture.** It is not a source-hardware board, but it is more reproducible and adaptable than many nominally open instruments. The source-location and USB-stem status need confirmation before `STRONG_PASS`.

#### Caveats / verification gaps

- The official GitHub source link was not independently resolved through the repository connector.
- Per-part USB stem status is described inconsistently across official pages.
- No independent audio-latency, xrun, boot-time or thermal measurements were found.
- Custom audio I/O and physical controls require a separate hardware phase.

#### Sources

- [Official project page](https://brume.aftertone.co/)
- [Developer guide](https://brume.aftertone.co/develop.html)
- [Reference manual](https://brume.aftertone.co/docs.html)

### 3) [TuriSc/Dodepan](https://github.com/TuriSc/Dodepan) — **PASS**

**Lane:** Expressive capacitive RP2350 instrument, synth, looper and MIDI controller

#### Technical summary

Dodepan is played through twelve capacitive metal pads and uses an MPU-6050 IMU to derive tap intensity, pitch bend and tilt-controlled filter movement. The current version targets Raspberry Pi Pico 2/RP2350, adds I²S audio through MAX98357, OLED/encoder UI, sixteen scales, editable user scales, nine factory instruments, four user slots and an event looper with diatonic transposition.

#### Why it matters

The interesting part is the control language, not the bill of materials. It combines handpan-like tapping, motion data, local synthesis and loop transposition in a portable instrument that can also act as a MIDI controller. That is exactly the kind of unusual but practical concept the recalibrated mine should stop filtering out.

#### Implementation highlights

- Pico 2/RP2350 firmware built with Pico SDK and distributed as UF2.
- Twelve MPR121 capacitive electrodes.
- MPU-6050 impact/tilt data mapped to velocity, pitch bend and cutoff.
- PRA32-U-derived direct digital synthesis engine with 46 editable parameters.
- Event looper with overdub/play/pause and diatonic transport.
- Built-in and user-defined scales, user presets and deferred flash persistence.
- BOM, schematic PDF, assembly guide and 3D-print/OpenSCAD enclosure assets.

#### Hardware and electronics notes

**Evidence split:**

- **Physical hardware:** verified through build photos/video and assembly documentation.
- **Schematic:** PNG/PDF source is published.
- **Editable EDA:** not verified; repository search exposed Gerber/drill outputs rather than the underlying KiCad schematic/PCB project.
- **Fabrication package:** Gerbers/drills and assembly guidance are present.
- **Firmware:** source, build instructions and UF2 path are present.
- **Licence:** root MIT licence is present, but its scope over hardware CAD/fabrication data is not explicit.

The MAX98357 amplified output is not a defined line output. The README explicitly recommends external attenuation before sensitive equipment. Any product derivative needs a proper headphone/line path, ESD protection, mute control and defined output impedance.

#### Platform relevance

- **Very high:** unusual expressive instrument/UI concept.
- **High:** RP2350 synthesis, capacitive sensing and IMU mapping.
- **High:** portable self-contained hardware with a low harness count.
- **Medium:** hardware modification because editable PCB source was not verified.

#### Adaptation ideas

1. Rebuild the audio section around a codec or DAC plus separate speaker and line/headphone outputs.
2. Preserve the twelve-pad geometry while experimenting with force sensing or per-pad pressure.
3. Use the IMU gesture mapping for delay freeze, grain position, spectral morph or feedback control.
4. Add USB MIDI/device audio and a standard TRS MIDI port.
5. Publish the editable PCB source and clarify hardware licensing.

#### Quick engineering assessment

**Robust `PASS` for concept and build value.** It is not a pristine source-hardware package, but it is a real instrument with an original interface, coherent firmware and a reproducible physical build.

#### Caveats / verification gaps

- Editable PCB source was not verified.
- Root MIT licence may not clearly cover hardware artifacts.
- Audio output requires redesign for line/headphone use.
- Known OLED glitch under simultaneous note activity and encoder updates remains.
